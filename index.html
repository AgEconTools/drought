    What LFP is

    LFP pays livestock producers for grazing losses from drought or fire. For drought, it covers privately owned or cash-leased grazing land in a county the Drought Monitor rates in severe drought (D2) or worse, long enough to meet the triggers below, during the pasture's normal grazing period. Eligible livestock include beef and dairy cattle, sheep and goats.

    Source: FSA's LFP program page and fact sheet (linked above); 7 CFR 1416.201-1416.204.

    What FSA requires of you

    • An acreage report (form FSA-578) for the grazing land, filed with your county FSA office by the county's acreage reporting date, usually July 15 for pasture. FSA requires it before it processes an LFP application; a late report is accepted for a fee if FSA can still verify the acreage. There is no enrollment or premium.
    • An application (form CCC-853) at your county FSA office. In FSA's words, "applications must be submitted by March 1 following the end of the calendar year in which the grazing loss occurred" (March 1 of the next year), with the other forms FSA's program page lists and your records of the livestock and of ownership or lease of the land.
    • If your average adjusted gross income is over $900,000, you aren't eligible unless at least 75% of it comes from farming, ranching or forestry.

    Source: 7 CFR 1416.205 and 1416.206; FSA handbooks 1-LFP (amendment 7, August 2025, before the 2026 trigger change) and 2-CP (acreage reports); 7 CFR part 1400 and FSA's income limit page.

    The drought triggers

    FSA counts drought in "payment months", from 1 to 5, by how bad and how long it is on the Drought Monitor on any part of the county during the pasture type's normal grazing period. Each category includes the worse ones ("D2" means D2, D3 or D4).

    Drought Monitor ratingPayment months
    D2 (severe) for 4 weeks in a row1
    D2 for 7 of 8 weeks2
    D3 (extreme) at any time3
    D3 for 4 weeks (not necessarily in a row), or D4 (exceptional) at any time4
    D4 for 4 weeks5

    FSA's weekly list has credited the second month only when the 8 weeks start with 4 weeks in a row of D2. This page follows FSA's list.

    FSA's weekly list shows each county's level by pasture type. These triggers apply from program year 2026 on; a July 2025 law cut the D2 trigger from 8 weeks to 4 and added the 7-of-8-weeks level.

    A CMOR report you file doesn't by itself change the map or your county's LFP level.

    Source: 7 U.S.C. 9081(c)(3)(D), as amended by Public Law 119-21 (July 4, 2025); 7 CFR 1416.205(a)(3) and 1416.207, as amended July 9, 2026; FSA's weekly list of eligible counties.

    Normal grazing periods

    FSA sets a normal grazing period for each pasture type in each county, and only drought weeks inside it count. A grazing period allows one payment month for each full 30 days it lasts, up to five. FSA's weekly list shows each county's pasture types, grazing periods and payment months. Your acreage report determines which pasture types apply to your land.

    Source: 7 CFR 1416.202 and 1416.205; FSA's weekly list of eligible counties.

    How FSA figures the payment

    • The monthly payment rate is 60% of the monthly feed cost for each animal. For an adult beef cow the feed cost is 15.7 pounds of corn a day for 30 days at the year's corn price; other livestock are converted to that with FSA's animal-unit factors. If the feed cost based on the land's normal carrying capacity is lower, that is used instead.
    • If you sold or otherwise disposed of livestock because of drought in either of the two previous years, you get 80% of the rate.
    • Payment = monthly rate × payment months × eligible animals. Payment months are a multiplier, not a schedule of checks: FSA pays after it processes the application, and pays the difference if the county reaches a higher level later in the grazing period.
    • Payments are limited to $125,000 per person or legal entity a program year.
    • Payments are reduced by sequestration, a budget-law cut that applies to most USDA farm programs. The dollar figures on this page are after that cut.

    Source: 7 U.S.C. 9081(c)(3)(B)-(C) and (f)(2); 7 CFR 1416.207; OMB's sequestration reports under the Balanced Budget and Emergency Deficit Control Act.

    Where this page's numbers come from

    • Drought: U.S. Drought Monitor county statistics (National Drought Mitigation Center at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln, USDA and NOAA), the share of each county's area in each category. A county's color is the worst category on any part of it, however small.
    • LFP: the drought triggers above, applied week by week. Grazing periods come from FSA's weekly list of eligible counties and, for counties not on it, FSA's grazing period records (obtained under FOIA and archived by the Montana Climate Office). Checked against FSA's weekly list, this page's payment months agreed for all but a handful of the nearly 16,000 county pasture types; where they differ, FSA's list is the official one.
    • Payment estimates use FSA's monthly feed cost for an adult beef cow.
    • Beef cows in drought weight each county's Census of Agriculture beef cows by the share of its land in drought, the way USDA counts livestock in drought. USDA's weekly Ag in Drought report gives the share of all cattle in drought; this page counts beef cows only, so the two differ.
    • County outlines: U.S. Census Bureau, 2024. The maps cover the 48 contiguous states and D.C.
