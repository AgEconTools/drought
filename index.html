    What LFP is

    In FSA's words, LFP "provides financial support to eligible livestock producers who experience grazing losses due to qualifying drought or fire conditions." For drought, "compensation is available for livestock producers with privately owned or cash-leased land in counties designated as experiencing qualifying drought conditions," as rated by the U.S. Drought Monitor during the pasture's normal grazing period. It covers the grazing livestock FSA lists as eligible, including beef and dairy cattle, sheep and goats.

    Producers have to apply for LFP at their county FSA office; what FSA requires of producers, including the application deadline, is below.

    Source: FSA's LFP program page and fact sheet (linked above); 7 CFR 1416.201-1416.204.

    What FSA requires of producers

    • An acreage report (form FSA-578) for the grazing land, on file with the county FSA office under the producer's farm number by the county's acreage reporting date, usually July 15 for pasture. FSA's handbook requires the acreage report before an LFP application is processed; a late report is accepted for a fee where FSA can still verify the acreage. There is no enrollment or premium.
    • An application (form CCC-853) at the county FSA office. In FSA's words, "applications must be submitted by March 1 following the end of the calendar year in which the grazing loss occurred" (March 1 of the next year), with the other forms FSA's program page lists and records of the livestock and of ownership or lease of the grazing land.

    FSA determines eligibility, grazing periods, carrying capacities and payments from its own records. This page does not; your county FSA office is the place to ask.

    Source: 7 CFR 1416.205 and 1416.206; FSA handbook 1-LFP (amendment 7, August 2025).

    The drought triggers

    FSA counts drought in "payment months", from 1 to 5, by how bad and how long it is on the Drought Monitor on any part of the county during the pasture type's normal grazing period. Each category includes the worse ones ("D2" means D2, D3 or D4).

    Drought Monitor ratingPayment months
    D2 (severe) for 4 weeks in a row1
    D2 for 7 of 8 weeks in a row (the first 4 in a row)2
    D3 (extreme) at any time3
    D3 for 4 weeks (not necessarily in a row), or D4 (exceptional) at any time4
    D4 for 4 weeks5

    A county reaches each level as the drought does, and FSA's weekly list of eligible counties records the level for each county and pasture type. These are the triggers for program year 2026 on; the Working Families Tax Cuts Act of July 4, 2025 lowered the D2 trigger from 8 weeks to 4 and added the 7-of-8-weeks trigger.

    The Drought Monitor's authors can see the reports producers file through the National Drought Mitigation Center's Condition Monitoring Observer Reports (CMOR). A report becomes part of the record, but it doesn't by itself change the map or a county's LFP level.

    Source: 7 U.S.C. 9081(c)(3); FSA's weekly list of eligible counties.

    Normal grazing periods

    FSA sets a normal grazing period for each pasture type in each county, and only drought weeks inside it count. A grazing period allows one payment month for each full 30 days it lasts, up to five. FSA's weekly list shows each county's pasture types, grazing periods and payment months; FSA's acreage records determine which pasture types apply to a producer's land.

    Source: 7 CFR 1416.202 and 1416.205; FSA's weekly list of eligible counties.

    How FSA figures the payment

    • The monthly payment rate is 60% of the monthly feed cost for each animal. For an adult beef cow the feed cost is 15.7 pounds of corn a day for 30 days at the year's corn price; other livestock are converted to that with FSA's animal-unit factors. If the feed cost based on the land's normal carrying capacity is lower, that is used instead.
    • The payment is the monthly rate times the payment months times the eligible animals. Payment months are a multiplier, not a schedule of checks: FSA pays after the application is processed, with the balance if the county reaches a higher level later in the grazing period.
    • Payments are limited to $125,000 per person or legal entity a program year.
    • Payments are reduced by sequestration, a budget-law cut that applies to most USDA farm programs. The dollar figures on this page are after that cut.

    Payments come only after an application; see what FSA requires of producers for the forms and the deadline.

    Source: 7 U.S.C. 9081(c)(4)-(5); 7 CFR 1416.205; OMB's sequestration reports under the Balanced Budget and Emergency Deficit Control Act.

    Where this page's numbers come from

    • Drought: U.S. Drought Monitor county statistics (National Drought Mitigation Center at the University of Nebraska-Lincoln, USDA and NOAA), the share of each county's area in each category. A county's color is the worst category on any part of it, however small.
    • LFP: the drought triggers above, applied week by week. Grazing periods come from FSA's weekly list of eligible counties and, for counties not on it, FSA's grazing period records (obtained under FOIA and archived by the Montana Climate Office). Checked against FSA's weekly list, this page's payment months agreed for all but a handful of the nearly 16,000 county pasture types; where they differ, FSA's list is the official one.
    • Payment estimates use FSA's monthly feed cost for an adult beef cow.
    • Beef cows in drought weight each county's Census of Agriculture beef cows by the share of its land in drought, the way USDA counts livestock in drought.
    • County outlines: U.S. Census Bureau, 2024. The maps cover the 48 contiguous states and D.C.
